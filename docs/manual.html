THE PRACTICAL GUIDE / BROWSER EDITION

Know the controls.
Hear the difference.

VirtuoX combines an FM-1-shaped browser instrument with a piano coach. This manual describes implemented browser behavior. Physical VirtuoX firmware is not released.

Start · MIDI · Learning · Scoring · Patches · Sound design · Sequencer · Hardware · Troubleshooting

1. Start with a sound.

  1. Open the browser instrument and click Switch on. Browser audio requires a user gesture.
  2. Play the panel keyboard. Computer keys Z–/ address the lower keys, Q–O the upper keys; black-key shortcuts follow the panel input mapping.
  3. Click Bank A–D, then drag or scroll PRESETS. The LCD shows neighboring patches, while the bank and slot remain visible below the panel.
  4. Select Piano Coach or DX7-Compatible Player. Your selected sound is shared between these modes.

The panel has 27 keys from F3 to G5 before octave shifting. OCT −/+ shifts the on-screen keyboard. Some lessons exceed this range and benefit from an external MIDI keyboard.

2. Connect a real keyboard.

  1. Use desktop Chrome or Edge on the HTTPS site.
  2. Connect a USB MIDI keyboard and choose Connect MIDI.
  3. Allow MIDI access and select the intended input from the list.
  4. Choose All channels or a specific channel in GLO → Instrument mode.

The app uses Web MIDI. A BLE keyboard may work if the operating system exposes it as an ordinary MIDI input; direct Web Bluetooth BLE MIDI pairing is not implemented. Latency depends on the keyboard, connection, browser and audio device. This is not a latency-free system.

Velocity-zero Note On is treated as Note Off; sustain pedal CC64 is supported. CC1 adjusts modulation depth. Selecting an FM-1 output for patch transfer is a separate operation from selecting your playing input.

3. Build your learning mix — Core Matrix.

Core Matrix names the musical curriculum. Open GLO → Learning, or click GLO Learning below the instrument. Choose Everything, one subject, or Custom mix. The four knobs control Learning plan, Mix topic, Include topic and Concept cards. The browser also provides six subject toggles; keep at least one selected.

  • Songs:arrangements and excerpts including Twinkle, Twinkle, Ode to Joy, Für Elise, Minuet in G, two-hand studies and original grooves.
  • Theory:note names, accidentals, half/whole steps, intervals, triads, inversions, scale degrees, I–IV–V–I harmony, note values, rests and guided interval listening.
  • Scales:12 major and 12 natural-minor keys, chromatic coordination, plus pentatonic, blues and harmonic minor.
  • Chords:triads, inversions, arpeggios, seventh, suspended and diminished chords and chord progressions.
  • Rhythm:subdivision and timing drills of increasing complexity.
  • Technique:five-finger coordination, thumb-under motion, dynamics, articulation and hand independence.

The current song or exercise name appears in the banner and on the LCD. Concept cards explain the musical idea, formula and task. Listen demonstrates the target. Singing suggestions are practice prompts; voice and ear-recognition answers are not assessed.

Pulse — build tempo between exercises

Turn on Adaptive tempo in GLO Challenge. The current implementation adjusts the next suggested tempo after an exercise: +5 BPM at an overall score of at least 90%, −5 BPM below 65%, otherwise unchanged, within 30–200 BPM. Explicit repeated loops can adopt that tempo; the automatic course also considers the next study’s tempo. Pulse does not continuously change the clock while you are playing a phrase.

Automatic course continuation starts the next count-in without another Play press. Next lesson skips ahead. Repeat selected bars is an explicit override. Choosing a narrow subject changes the course; incompatible hand filters can leave no available studies.

4. Understand the score.

Complete chord first.All required tones must be physically held together. Releasing a partial attempt clears those provisional notes. Single-key taps cannot accumulate chord credit, and extra uncredited held keys block an exact-chord hit.

Timed mode:each onset must be inside the configured window. Chord spread must not exceed the smaller of 100 ms and the timing window. Feedback uses the worst onset error. Wait mode:the lesson waits for the full chord and timing is ungraded.

Timed note units earn Perfect 250, Great 180 or Good 120 points; wait-mode success earns Nice 100 without a timing claim. Complete chords earn points weighted by note count and one combo hit. Multipliers rise at 8, 16 and 32 complete hits. Mistakes break the combo without deleting points already earned.

The result also reports pitch, timing, note length and dynamics where enabled. Missed notes affect timing percentages rather than leaving a misleading 100% from the few correct notes. Note-length and dynamics grading are optional. Visual finger suggestions do not establish correct fingering or posture.

5. Banks, presets and your own sounds.

The public edition includes 128 M-VAVE FM-1 V15 factory voices in original slot order. Bank buttons select A–D and load the first sound; PRESETS wraps through the selected bank’s 32 voices. While the preset list is open, ALGORITHM changes banks. The popup disappears after five seconds without key activity; held keys keep it visible. ENV/LFO or an edit knob dismiss it immediately.

The browser engine uses strict DX7 parameter ranges. Twelve parameters from the factory source are adjusted in the browser copy. The original hash-verified VMEM and the exact adjustment log are included under third-party/m-vave/. The browser bank files are the adapted versions.

  1. Open Sounds and choose Import your own .syx.
  2. Choose a single-voice or 32-voice DX7 SysEx file. Invalid or unsupported voices are rejected.
  3. Select and audition imported voices. Favorite useful patches.
  4. Download the edited DX7 voice or a 32-slot favorites bank for backup.

Importing stays local to the browser; it does not publish your files. The public repository excludes the personal Yamaha ROM/Dexed collection. Imported sounds and custom songs persist where browser storage permits; export important work independently.

6. Use DX7-Compatible Player as an instrument.

Player mode hides lesson controls, scores and automatic teaching. HOME gives four sound macros. PRESETS changes sound; ALGORITHM changes the FM algorithm in Player or EDIT unless the preset list is using it for bank selection.

  • EDIT / FM:brightness, feedback, carrier detune, filter cutoff, engine selection, algorithm and six operator pages.
  • ENV:Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release. DX7 rate values increase speed; analog time values increase duration. Envelope changes apply to subsequent notes.
  • LFO:speed, modulation depth, wave and sensitivity; analog mode offers pitch, filter or both as targets.
  • FX:Mix, Reverb, Delay, Chorus and Filter/Drive pages.
  • Analog-inspired engine:Ladder Bass, Singing Lead, Warm Triangle, Pulse Pluck, Wide Saw Pad and Acid Step. These are separate subtractive sounds, not exact Moog models or DX7-exportable voices.
  • ARP:in Player, enable the arpeggiator and hold notes. Choose Up, Down, Up/Down or Random; quarter/eighth/sixteenth note rates; one to three octaves.

7. Make the sequencer play.

  1. Open Compose or press SEQ.
  2. Click Load example groove for an immediate starting pattern.
  3. Click Run sequence. The purple playhead shows the sounding step. Click again to stop.
  4. To write your own pattern, select a numbered step and play notes or a chord. Select another step and continue. Empty steps are rests.
  5. Use the four controls for step selection, length (16/32/64), gate and swing. The second page includes tempo and velocity.
  6. Clear a selected step, clear the whole pattern, or toggle looping. Changes to tempo and pattern are read at the next cycle.

Repeated pitches are retriggered even when swing makes neighboring gates overlap. Stale note-off timers cannot cut a later retrigger. Stop cancels pending events and releases held sequence notes. The browser Run/Stop button and the physical-style PLAY button use the same playback function.

In Coach, Sequence → lesson turns the pattern into a target exercise. REC records live performance; Recording → lesson uses its note events. Export a lesson JSON to retain it outside browser storage. The live recording buffer and current sequence are session-only.

8. Transfer a DX7 voice to an FM-1.

The browser’s FM-1 ↔ dialog sends patch parameters, not VirtuoX teaching firmware. Enable SysEx, select the explicit output and response input, and select the firmware actually installed.

  • Stock V16:standard Yamaha single-voice SysEx to the edit buffer. Delivery is unacknowledged. Store the voice using the device’s own save operation.
  • JIANT/Felucca:capability detection and an acknowledged FM6 command to the selected track; the track must already be FM6.
  • Unknown firmware:do not guess the protocol. Identify firmware and back up before transfer.

Browser effects and analog sounds are not sent with a DX7 patch. Whole bank download is supported; the app does not send a bank-overwrite command. Protocol tests are not a substitute for physical hardware validation.

9. If something does not work.

No sound?

Click Switch on and play a key. Check browser/tab and system volume. Use headphones or speakers connected to the computer: the demo’s audio is synthesized in the browser. MIDI input does not automatically route browser audio through the FM-1.

The sequencer is silent?

Open Compose, load the example groove and click Run sequence. A blank pattern contains only rests. If you were auditioning a patch, open SEQ to dismiss the preset popup before entering notes. A moving playhead without sound points to browser audio or output routing.

MIDI keyboard missing?

Use Chrome/Edge, connect the keyboard before refreshing the port list, allow MIDI permission and select the intended input. Check the channel filter. BLE devices must be made available as OS MIDI ports first; direct BLE pairing is not provided here.

Expected patch or menu missing?

The public edition includes FM-1 factory banks, not the private Yamaha archive. Analog / Moog-style opens the separate analog engine. The browser app is not the complete JIANT firmware and does not include all of JIANT’s engines and project features.

Wrong chord or timing score?

Check the target notes, octave, transpose, input channel and timing window. Hold every chord tone together; avoid extra held keys. In timed mode, both onset accuracy and chord spread matter. Test a simple triad in the standalone chord-scoring page and include a reproducible example in an issue.

10. Report an issue that can be reproduced.

Include browser/OS, MIDI keyboard, mode, patch bank/slot, lesson or sequence steps, BPM, swing/gate settings, expected behavior and what happened. Include whether the on-screen keyboard reproduces it. Do not attach private patch banks unless you have permission to share them.

Style library & expressive synthesizer practice

In Piano coach, open Style library. Choose M Dean inspired, Jarre inspired, Moog fundamentals, Mozart, Bach or Classical. The 86 original studies include automatic sound recipes and MIDI downloads. Twelve attributed Mutopia MIDI editions offer first-eight-bar practice or complete loading; select another sound to practice classical music on a synth.

For M Dean-inspired practice, begin with bass roots and octaves, then syncopation, mono lead and legato, spacious chord voicings and performance builds. Pitch bend, filtering and wheel movements are guided activities; their gestures are not automatically scored.

Player navigation & projects

DX7-Compatible Player removes coaching controls from GLO and repurposes SCL as Keyboard & wheels and SAVE as Projects. Use SCL to select mono/poly, legato, octave and bend range; ENV includes analog glide. Bend currently affects the analog engine only. A real MIDI mod wheel controls LFO depth. Save a project to retain custom sound parameters, effects and sequence together, or export JSON for a backup.

Player is still a subset of the complete native firmware. Read the source review and exact feature comparison, or open the separate official FM1 Open studio from the Player toolbar.

Player LCD & direct sound editing

HOME shows the actual output waveform and dBFS level, individual held notes, key velocity and four sound macros. No operator activity is simulated. EDIT shows programmed operator levels, not audio meters. ENV, LFO and FX use different diagrams with live parameter values; envelope and effect curves are schematic rather than measurements.

In FM Player ENV, SELECT chooses Quick envelope, Operator rates, Operator levels, Pitch rates or Pitch levels. On operator pages, ALGORITHM chooses OP1–OP6; the four top-right knobs edit R1–R4 or L1–L4. The browser also offers six direct operator buttons. Quick envelope still edits carriers together. All these edits are included in exported DX7 SysEx.

LFO now has Pitch & waveform, Amplitude & delay, and Sync & response pages. Amplitude modulation sensitivity applies to the selected operator. The sync page controls LFO key sync, oscillator sync, pitch sensitivity and voice transpose. SEQ shows the selected 16-step bank as a piano roll with the real playhead. PLAY starts an existing sequence from any Player sound page without leaving that page; press again to stop.

SAVE first opens Projects. Press SAVE again to store the selected slot; PLAY loads that slot. Knob 1 selects one of eight slots. JSON backup controls remain below the instrument.

Automatic style sounds

Click Learn a style in the header, choose a style, then Study. Keep Recommended sound for the study selected to load matching sound and FX automatically. As the course advances, its next study selects its own recommended sound. Choose Keep current sound to retain your instrument, or select a fixed recipe to use across the path.

Draik is a stylized label for 20 original late-night R&B song tracks: velvet minor-seven keys, phrase space, sub-bass, ninth voicings, floating pads and a keys/top-line sketch. Dok Dree offers 20 original West Coast funk song tracks: rounded bass, glide lead, chord stabs, sixteenth pickups, minor changes and a lead turnaround. The names are stylized references, not affiliations, endorsements or artist song transcriptions.

Choose a learning style using only the LCD

In Piano Coach, press GLO, then turn SELECT to page 8 — Learning style. Knob 1 selects M Dean inspired, Jarre inspired, Moog fundamentals, Draik, Dok Dree, Mozart, Bach or Classical. Knob 2 selects a study or MIDI edition; knob 3 selects difficulty; knob 4 sets tempo. The LCD shows the full lesson name, lesson position and recommended sound. PLAY begins that lesson. Mozart and Bach MIDI files load automatically; the first eight bars are selected for practice. GLO page 7 still switches instrument mode, and Player retains seven synth-only pages.

Hear a style demo

On the LCD Learning style page, press REC to hear the selected track with matching sound and effects. REC again stops it; PLAY begins your selected lesson. The top style preview plays up to four bars twice, without scoring or recording, and keep your selected lesson intact. The library and Learning style page also offer Demo selected style buttons. The top style-preview button uses a curated example; REC and individual track cards demo the selected track. Demo sound changes are replaced by the lesson’s recommended sound when you start practice from the LCD selector.

Twenty tracks per artist-inspired path

M Dean inspired, Jarre inspired, Draik and Dok Dree each offer 20 distinct original song tracks. Tracks run 8, 12 or 16 bars with Theme A, Theme B, bridge and coda sections as appropriate. The music draws on broad production techniques and sound design rather than copying artist melodies. Difficulty levels progress from 1 to 5. Use the bar loop to learn one four-bar section, then play the whole arrangement.

Turn knob 2 on GLO → Learning style to select any of the 20 tracks. REC demos that entire selected track once; REC again stops it. PLAY starts practice. In the browser library, every card has Demo track and a MIDI download. Complete classical editions have Demo complete piece; the top Demo selected style button plays a short preview. Bach includes four complete MIDI pieces: Invention No. 1 BWV 772, Invention No. 2 BWV 773, Prelude BWV 846, and Prelude BWV 933.

Keyboard range

FM-1 range (default): all lesson targets and style demos stay on the fixed 27-key F3–G5 panel. Whole octave shifts preserve intervals when possible; wide scores use compact octave voicings and may merge doubled notes. GLO → Practice flow → knob 4, or Practice setup → Keyboard range, selects Original register for external keyboards. Original MIDI downloads are preserved.

Demos and sound libraries

Choose Installed sound library in Sounds or Styles, or GLO → Instrument mode → knob 3. Automatic lesson sounds select real patches from that installed set, varying by track and musical role. Explicit analog recipes and Keep current sound remain available. Style demos continue through the selected style and wrap until stopped; disable Continue demos for a single track. Listen also continues through the current learning path. Drag a knob around its rim clockwise to increase or counterclockwise to decrease; dragging from its center supports up/right to increase and down/left to decrease.
